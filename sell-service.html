3. The stack buyers expect (and what you can deliver)

A professional shorts-repurposing setup in 2026 has four layers. Here is what each one does and what it costs at the free tier:

Layer 1: Script/voiceover

Tool: Fish Audio TTS (free tier: s2.1-pro-free model).
Output: MP3 per scene, Indonesian or English, ~150 words/min.
Cost: $0. API key required (free).

Layer 2: B-roll / visuals

Sources: Pexels API (free, no attribution required) + Pixabay API (free).
Output: MP4 clips, 1080p or 4K.
Cost: $0. Pexels needs a browser User-Agent header (Cloudflare blocks bare curl). Pixabay per_page must be ≥3.

Layer 3: Assembly + rendering

Tool: Remotion (React-based, free and open source) OR ffmpeg + Python.
Output: 16:9 horizontal MP4 (for long-form) OR 9:16 vertical MP4 (for Shorts).
Cost: $0. Renders in ~20-60s per 60-second clip on a laptop GPU.

Layer 4: Vertical conversion (the core service step)

Tool: ffmpeg zoom-pan filter chain.
Effect: Source centered full-width, background blurred + darkened, burned-in
subtitles. Output: 1080×1920 vertical MP4.
Cost: $0. ~15-45s per clip.

The entire stack — TTS + stock + assembly + vertical — runs on a single laptop with no cloud costs. The marginal cost of each deliverable is electricity + a few seconds of GPU.

6. Delivery workflow (from order to MP4)

  1. Intake: Buyer submits requirements via the GitHub issue form (5 questions: source material, number of clips, language, duration, style).
  2. Script: If the source is an article or script, paste it. If raw footage, extract the audio and summarize.
  3. Voiceover: Generate per-scene MP3 via Fish Audio TTS. ~30 seconds.
  4. B-roll: Pull relevant clips from Pexels/Pixabay via API. ~1-2 minutes.
  5. Render: Remotion or ffmpeg assembles scenes into a 16:9 horizontal. ~20-60s.
  6. Vertical cut: ffmpeg zoom-pan converts to 9:16 with burned-in subs. ~15-45s.
  7. QA: Human review of the output. ~2-5 minutes.
  8. Delivery: MP4 file via Google Drive, WeTransfer, or direct download link.
  • Invoice: PayPal for international, direct bank transfer for Indonesia.

    • Total wall-clock time per clip: ~5-10 minutes of human attention. Total render time: ~2 minutes. At scale (a 20-clip retainer), you batch the renders overnight and deliver in the morning.

    8. Getting the first client (the cold-start)

    The hardest part is not delivering — it is getting the first order. Three approaches that work:

    Method 1: The storefront link

    A live page with a demo reel, pricing, and a zero-friction order form (GitHub issue) converts a visitor into a buyer with no signup. Share the link in relevant communities.

    Method 2: Direct outreach to small YouTubers

    Find channels with 1k-50k subscribers who publish long-form but no Shorts. DM them: "I noticed you are not on Shorts. I can turn your existing videos into vertical clips — here is a free sample." The sample is your demo reel.

    Method 3: Marketplace listing (once KYC is done)

    Paste the gig copy, link to the live storefront as your portfolio, and set a low introductory price for the first 3-5 reviews. After 5 reviews, raise to market rate.

    9. Scaling beyond yourself

    Once you have 3-5 retainer clients, you are at ~$1,000-$1,250/mo with ~100 clips/month. Your bottleneck is not render time — it is client communication and QA. Options:

    • Hire a VA for intake and communication (you keep the pipeline).
    • Productize further: offer a self-serve toolkit (Gumroad, $29) for people who want to do it themselves — the same stack, documented.
    • Specialize: become the go-to person for a specific niche (tech news, education, product demos).
